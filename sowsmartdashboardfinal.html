Argentina soybean climate analysis · 2022/23 and 2024/25

October 1, 2022 to April 30, 2023. The Buenos Aires pilot had 19 days at or above 35 °C between January 1 and March 15, versus 1.2 days on average over the same dates in the 27 preceding seasons. The project’s national soybean series shows production falling from 43.9 to 25.0 million tonnes between the preceding crop and the 2022/23 drought crop.

October 1, 2024 to April 30, 2025. USDA reported below-normal January rainfall across the major soybean provinces and some relief in early February. Use this as a second stress case alongside the more severe 2022/23 national production decline. USDA February 2025 outlook

Irrigation and lender follow-up

INTA research in northern Buenos Aires highlights water deficits during pod formation and early seed filling (R3–R5). When heat and low moisture overlap, confirm the field’s crop stage, whether it is rainfed or irrigated, and whether usable water and equipment are available. Ask an agronomist to verify field moisture and irrigation timing. Radar’s gridded values cannot verify water access or irrigation actually applied. INTA soybean water-deficit research

Heat and rain: Radar’s embedded pilot series at 34.06°S, 61.20°W. Its upstream dataset is unspecified in the saved page. The heat baseline is 1995/96–2021/22, excluding leap day. Soil wetness: supplied NASA POWER MERRA-2 GWETROOT at 34.00°S, 61.25°W (0–1). The 2022/23 soil file covers Oct 15–Mar 15 and the 2024/25 file covers Oct 15–Apr 15. The replays are retrospective.

Loan detail

Loan portfolio is simulated (loan_master.csv and monthly_snapshot.csv, 200 soybean loans). Climate classes use observed climate data. Expected loss is illustrative, not model output. Cover terms are simulated: cover % of the loss after a 10% deductible, capped at $50,000 per loan. Monthly stress is a hypothetical seasonal ramp. Areas span Buenos Aires, Santa Fe, Córdoba, La Pampa and Entre Ríos. Climate class: drought if Jan–Mar rainfall is under 75% of normal; vegetation stress if Jan–Mar NDVI is more than 8% below the 10-year median; otherwise low. Measures harvest risk, not default.
